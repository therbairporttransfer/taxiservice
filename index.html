Landing Late at Stansted: Train, Coach or Private Transfer into London?

Stansted is busy well into the night, but London's onward transport is not. Here is how the choice changes once your flight lands after dark.

By {{AUTHOR_NAME}} · {{DATE_PUBLISHED}}

The short answer: if you clear arrivals in good time for the last Stansted Express and are staying near Liverpool Street or Tottenham Hale, the train is usually the simplest option. After the last train, coaches are the only public transport into London, and they run through the night. If you are travelling with children, heavy luggage or a group, or staying somewhere awkward to reach late at night, a pre-booked car is often the least tiring way to finish the journey.

London Stansted sits about 40 miles north-east of central London, off the M11 in Essex. In the daytime, that distance barely matters, because trains and coaches leave every few minutes. Late at night it matters a great deal. The question is no longer just how to reach London. It is how to reach your actual bed, and whether the connections you need at the other end are still running.

Why your landing time changes everything

Most advice about Stansted ground transport assumes a mid-afternoon arrival. Three things change as the evening goes on.

  • The train stops. The Stansted Express runs roughly every 15 minutes during the day, but the last departure from the airport is around half past midnight, according to the airport's own guidance.
  • The second leg gets harder. Reaching Liverpool Street is only half the trip. Most Underground lines close around midnight. The Night Tube runs only on Friday and Saturday nights and only on certain lines, so on other nights you are relying on night buses or a taxi.
  • Your energy drops. After a delayed flight, a long passport queue and a wait for bags, a plan with two or three changes feels very different at 1am than it looked on paper.

A useful way to think about it is in three time bands, based on when you expect to walk out of the terminal, not when the plane lands.

The last trains: Stansted Express

The Stansted Express runs from the station beneath the terminal to London Liverpool Street, calling at Tottenham Hale. The airport quotes about 48 minutes to Liverpool Street and around 36 minutes to Tottenham Hale. Trains have luggage space, and the station is a short walk from arrivals.

Late at night, two details matter more than journey time:

  • Where you change. Tottenham Hale is on the Victoria line. On Friday and Saturday nights, the Night Tube's Victoria line can carry you on into central and south London. Liverpool Street connects to the Central line, which also runs a Night Tube service on those nights. On weeknights, both stations mean a night bus or a taxi rank.
  • Engineering works. The West Anglia line is regularly closed at weekends for maintenance, with rail replacement buses instead. These take longer and carry fewer people. Check National Rail for your travel date before you rely on the last train.

The train suits solo travellers and couples with manageable bags, landing comfortably before the last departure and staying near one of the two London stops.

After midnight: the overnight coaches

Coaches are the one form of public transport that keeps running all night. Stansted's coach station is a couple of minutes' walk from the terminal. National Express operates several London routes, and Flibco advertises departures every 30 minutes, around the clock, to Liverpool Street, Stratford and Finsbury Park. Between them, the operators serve stops including Victoria, Liverpool Street, Stratford and Baker Street.

Coaches are usually the cheapest way into London and need no booking for a specific train. The trade-offs show up at night: the journey depends on M11 traffic and roadworks, you still need onward transport from the drop-off point, and stepping off at Victoria or Stratford at 2am with three suitcases is not everyone's idea of the end of a holiday.

For travellers on a budget who are used to night travel and staying near a coach stop, coaches are a sensible fallback that does not disappear when the trains stop.

Door to door: taxis and pre-booked transfers

There is an official taxi rank outside the terminal. At busy times, or very late, you may find a queue. Fares from the rank are not fixed in advance, so check the likely cost for your destination before you get in.

The alternative is to arrange a car before you fly. For travellers with several suitcases, young children, or a destination that would take two or three connections at night, a pre-booked Stansted Airport transfer removes every change. You walk from arrivals to a car that knows where you are going. This is most useful when your destination is outside central London (Hertfordshire, north-east London or Cambridge, say) where late-night public transport is thin.

When you book any private hire car, give the operator your flight number rather than just a time. Ask how long the driver will wait after landing at no extra charge, and confirm where you will meet. At Stansted, pre-booked drivers usually collect from the terminal car parks rather than the forecourt, so the meeting point is worth knowing before your phone battery runs low.

Which option suits which traveller?

There is no single winner. The right choice depends on who is travelling, what they are carrying and where they are going.

Mistakes travellers make after a late landing

  1. Planning from the scheduled landing time. Taxiing, disembarking, passport control and reclaim can easily add an hour or more. A flight due at 23:15 does not mean you will be on the 23:30 train.
  2. Forgetting the second leg. Getting to Liverpool Street is not the same as getting to Clapham or Wembley. Check how the final stretch works at that hour on that day of the week.
  3. Ignoring weekend engineering works. A rail replacement bus can turn a 48-minute train into a much longer trip, with a change on the way.
  4. Booking a car without a flight number. If the flight is delayed, a driver working from a fixed time may have moved on.
  5. Leaving accommodation check-in to chance. Some hotels and holiday lets have late-arrival rules. Tell them you are arriving after midnight.

Making a late arrival easier

  • Save the timetables and your booking confirmations offline before you fly, and keep your phone charged for the walk out.
  • Buy train or coach tickets in advance where you can, so you are not queuing at a machine at midnight.
  • If you are deciding between the last train and a coach, choose before you land. A plan you have to rethink in the reclaim hall is a plan that costs you time.
  • Travelling with children? Pack a small bag with snacks, a change of clothes and anything they need to sleep in the car or on the coach.
  • On weekends, check whether the Night Tube runs on the line you need from Tottenham Hale or Liverpool Street. Its timetable is on TfL.

Timetables, coach routes and airport pick-up arrangements change. Check London Stansted Airport's bus and coach guidance and the operator for your actual travel date.

Final thoughts

Stansted works well for late flights, as long as you plan the whole journey and not just the first 40 miles. If you will be out of the terminal before the last train and staying near its stops, the Stansted Express is hard to beat. If the train has gone, the overnight coaches keep you moving for little money. If you are tired, laden or heading somewhere public transport serves badly at night, a car waiting at arrivals is worth considering.

Frequently asked questions

What time is the last Stansted Express to London?

The airport says the last departure to Liverpool Street is around 00:30, but times vary on public holidays and during engineering works. Check the timetable for your date.

Is there public transport from Stansted to London through the night?

Yes. Coach services run 24 hours a day to several London stops, including Liverpool Street and Stratford. There are no trains between the last evening departure and the first morning service.

What happens if my flight lands after the last train?

Your options are an overnight coach, a taxi from the rank, or a car you booked in advance. If you book in advance, give the operator your flight number so the pick-up follows your actual landing time.

Is a private transfer worth it for a family arriving late?

Often, yes. A direct car avoids station changes and night buses with sleepy children and luggage. Request child seats when you book.
